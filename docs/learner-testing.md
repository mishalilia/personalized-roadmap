# Learner testing

Five learners outside the team used DotPath in November 2026. This page is the protocol and what came out of it.
Nothing that identifies a learner is in this repository.

## What we wanted to learn

1. Are the onboarding questions clear, and how long do they take?
2. Do learners agree with the suggested level and the decisions in their path?
3. Do checkpoints and coding problems feel fair, and does a failed checkpoint make sense?
4. Would they keep using the path?

## The session (about 25 minutes)

| Step | The learner | We note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answers the onboarding questions, thinking aloud | Time, questions misread |
| 2 | Reads the suggested level and its evidence | Agrees or changes it, and why |
| 3 | Reads the path, changes what they disagree with, confirms | Each change and its reason |
| 4 | Opens their first module and takes its checkpoint and coding problem | Score, unclear questions |
| 5 | Reads what changed after a failure, or the suggestions after a pass | Whether it makes sense |

Before the session the learner is told what is collected and that their answers go to the model provider.
Notes use a code (L1–L5), not a name.

## Results

- **Decisions:** the suggested decisions matched what the learners knew — modules they knew were marked to skip
  or prove, the rest to study.
- **What we changed after the sessions:**
  - the open onboarding questions became choices, each with optional details;
  - the path panel can be widened by dragging its edge;
  - the coding problem's editor behaves like an IDE;
  - smaller interface fixes.

No structured notes were kept; the results above are the team's summary of the sessions.
